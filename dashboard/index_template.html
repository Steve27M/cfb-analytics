Predictions you can check

Every FBS game of the season was predicted before it was played, frozen in public where it cannot be quietly edited, and scored against reality ever since.

Most football models are published after the fact, with the misses left out. This one commits first and keeps the receipts: the original forecast is sealed in git with a SHA-256 manifest, an updated model runs beside it, and both answer to the same scoreboard all season.

Why you can trust the numbers

Committed before kickoff

The preseason forecast for all 740 games was sealed on 1 July with a SHA-256 manifest. A model improved mid-season is scored forward only — no version ever gets credit for a game it could already see the result of.

Checked against an official source

Structural tests prove a number is well-formed, not that it is right — total offense here was 3% high for months and passed all of them. Every counting stat is now compared team-by-team against the official season totals before publication, and a failure stops the build.

Bad data is withheld, not published

When the in-season play-by-play feed disagreed with the scoreboard — its expected-points margins named the actual winner in just 54% of games — the affected metrics were pulled from the 2026 pages rather than shipped. They return automatically when the feed recovers.

Built twice, on purpose

Every model is implemented in both R and Python and the build fails if the two disagree beyond a tight tolerance. It is a slow way to write a model and a fast way to find a mistake.

What this site does not claim. It is descriptive analytics, not betting advice. The models page tests the question directly and reports a negative result: against closing lines the forecast scores 0.191 to the market's 0.176, and on games where the two disagree the market is right about 60% of the time. Picking winners at roughly 75% sounds impressive and is close to what picking the home team achieves; the honest measure is calibration, and the market is still better calibrated than this model.
